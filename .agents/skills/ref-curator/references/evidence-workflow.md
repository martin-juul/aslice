# Evidence workflow

## Establish the claim and edition

Read each citing section, not merely its link. List exact supported claims and
link each to its local section under `Used by`. Search local ref links and original
URLs throughout the project for consumers and duplicates. Distinguish dependencies
from examples, navigation, and background reading. Track uncaptured dependencies
in the inventory rather than implying their parent source includes them.

Identify publisher, authors, date, revision, and source type. Prefer immutable
release, commit, draft, or historical revision URLs. A branch URL is a dated
snapshot. Do not invent identifiers or assume today's page proves earlier content.

## Retrieve and compare

Use available web readers or HTTP tools; no particular connector is required.
Inspect the content, not only HTTP status: an Apple forum response has returned
HTTP 200 with a security-verification page. Loaded discussions can hide replies.
Record UTC retrieval date, method, resolved URL, revision, and success, partial
capture, or failure.

If unavailable, try appropriate publisher/raw/revision endpoints or historical
archives, labeling the fallback and edition. Stop when available routes cannot
recover the evidence; retain prior captures and record failed URL/date/error and
missing evidence. Never silently substitute a publisher or edition.

Check each claim and quotation against the actual source. Follow decision-relevant
dependencies where necessary; record those still missing. Verify section locators
against the retrieved edition, not merely the existence of a URL.

## Capture context and history

Apply the template's depth without a fixed word count: relevant mechanisms,
prerequisites, parameters/defaults, examples, outputs, exceptions, failures,
recovery, and alternatives. Separate source facts, quotes, paraphrases, local
examples, and project inference. Preserve negation, uncertainty, quantities, and
the force of requirements.

For discussions, retain relevant participants/roles, available dates and comment
permalinks, arguments/replies, disagreements, corrections, alternatives, and
outcomes. Separate experience reports from confirmed behavior and proposals from
shipped changes. Closed issues do not explain closure; accepted replies do not
establish consensus. Identify unrecovered replies, dates, and chronology.

Compare editions before editing. Keep old assets under their existing filenames;
give new editions distinct revision filenames and explain the relationship.
Document corrections with evidence without altering originals. Preserve conflicting
accounts for a separate project decision, not an incidental implementation change.

## Preserve assets and rights

Identify permissions applicable to each asset, not just its website or a linked
software repository. Retain required attribution, license, and copyright notices.
Unknown copying permission calls for an attributed factual account and an explicit
full-source gap, not wholesale copying.

Record asset filename, retrieval URL/date, revision, transformations (including
none), and SHA-256. Preserve original bytes; store extracted/rendered derivatives
separately with their relationship documented. Upstream links retain upstream
meaning. Full capture does not include linked dependencies unless separately saved.

## Review offline and reconcile

Without opening URLs, can a reader recover each claim, its conditions, reasoning,
limits, and competing evidence? Check supporting assets and notices. Record what
was omitted and why, and what missing evidence still blocks implementation claims.

Give each record one primary catalog entry in the best-fitting existing subject,
alphabetized by displayed title without regard to case. Cross-link related records.
Update inventory coverage, gaps, provenance, and affected counts. Maintain stable
filenames and incoming links when consolidating or superseding; retain historical
records with explicit successors. Do not automatically delete unused material.

Run mechanical checks and the prose preservation audit. Inspect the diff for
changed quotations, historical dates, notices, source bytes, and accidental project
decision changes. Report unresolved conflicts separately.
